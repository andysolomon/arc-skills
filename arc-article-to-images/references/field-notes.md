## Two compositions

**Cover:** small series identifier and rule; oversized short headline; one large original ink/crosshatch metaphor; a serif takeaway; at most three brief supporting lines; quiet source/brand footer. Reserve cobalt for one meaningful detail and an emphasis mark. Let the metaphor teach the idea, rather than merely decorate it.

**Lesson:** quiet masthead and rule; cobalt number badge and topic label; short heavy headline; one italic explanatory sentence; a large central diagram; one or two practical takeaway lines; footer rule and source label. A useful vertical allocation is 8% masthead, 17% title/subtitle, 45% visual, 20% takeaway, 10% footer. Treat this as a starting grid; fit the actual copy.

Aim for 35–65 words per lesson, excluding source metadata. A few strong labels and a visible relationship work better than paragraphs inside boxes. Use breathing room, consistent alignment, and thin dividers. Avoid dashboard tiles, gradients, glossy 3D, decorative icons, and multiple competing accent colors in this preset.

## Match the visual to the idea

| Source relationship | Visual grammar |
| --- | --- |
| Feedback changes the next action | Directed loop with a labeled exit |
| Selection within a limit | Inputs flowing into a bounded container |
| Repeatable improvement | Before → intervention → after, with return path |
| Evidence-gated choice | Decision branch with distinct yes/no outcomes |
| Measured trend | Chart from actual source data, with units and scale |
| Conceptual movement | Explicitly labeled schematic, no invented measurements |
| Tradeoff | Two comparable treatments on the same axes or baseline |

Make cobalt mean one thing per diagram: selected evidence, active path, changed item, or focal point. Arrows must express actual direction or sequence. Do not imply causality when the article only describes association. Label invented metaphors or schematic curves as illustrative when they could be mistaken for evidence.

Use the article title, supplied brand, or a neutral series label in the masthead. A source footer is attribution, not an endorsement. Keep long URLs in the accompanying source notes.

## Art direction

Before rendering, name the action the illustration explains: what enters, what changes, and what leaves. Choose a subject-specific object or mechanism over generic gears, brains, or decorative technology symbols. Give a cover one dominant object with a clear silhouette; use selective cutaways, perspective, and crosshatching when they reveal something useful.

Keep a series consistent through type, margins, and ink treatment while varying the central composition to fit each idea. An annotated phrase, aligned signal strips, or an interrupted path can teach more directly than identical boxes on every card. Use controlled stroke variation on illustration contours and callouts; keep data geometry, arrow endpoints, and type precise.

Compose generated artwork on the actual page background before judging it. Use transparent artwork when placing isolated objects on paper; check for rectangular color seams and alpha halos. Reserve cobalt for the mechanism being explained. Inspect at phone width so detailed engraving supports a strong silhouette instead of becoming visual noise.
